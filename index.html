Section A: Analytical and Geographical Reasoning

1. Coastal Regions Cooler: Coastal regions experience relatively cooler days due to the moderating effect of the sea. Water heats up and cools down slower than land, influencing temperature extremes. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

2. Aravalli Failure: The Aravalli Hills fail to intercept monsoon winds effectively because they run parallel to the direction of the southwest monsoon winds, allowing the moisture-laden air to pass by without shedding much rain. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

3. Khadar vs. Bangar:

  • Khadar is the new, younger alluvial soil, generally found in the flood plains, which is highly fertile and suitable for intensive agriculture.
  • Bangar is the older alluvial soil, found away from the flood plains, which is less fertile, often containing calcareous nodules, influencing crop selection towards less water-intensive varieties.
— Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

4. Black Soil & Cotton: Black soil is rich in minerals and has a high clay content, which gives it an excellent capacity for moisture retention. Cotton requires high temperatures, light rainfall (or irrigation), and a large number of frost-free days for proper growth. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

5. Chennai and Varanasi Temp Range: Chennai, being coastal, experiences a lower annual temperature range due to a maritime climate. Varanasi, being inland, experiences a higher annual temperature range due to a continental climate. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

6. Tank Irrigation Reasons: Tank irrigation is most prevalent in the peninsular region because the topography consists of hard, impermeable rock, which is ideal for collecting and storing water. Additionally, the seasonal nature of rainfall (monsoon dependence) necessitates the collection of water during wet months. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

7. Chota Nagpur Plateau Hub: The Chota Nagpur Plateau region has become a hub of the steel industry due to its proximity to vital raw materials like iron ore and coal. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

8. Silk Industry in Karnataka: The silk industry in Karnataka is well developed partly because the region provides favourable climatic conditions for rearing silkworms. Furthermore, the industry benefits from the traditional skills and labour availability in the region. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

9. Limitations of Transplantation:

  • Transplantation is extremely labour-intensive, requiring a high density of workers, which can be costly and challenging in areas facing rural labour shortages.
  • The method is time-consuming and often limits the rapid adoption of modern technology necessary for large-scale efficient farming.
— Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

10. Hindrances to Mechanization: Rural poverty means farmers often lack the capital needed to invest in expensive machinery and modern equipment. Land fragmentation (small, scattered landholdings) makes the use of large agricultural machinery economically unviable and physically difficult. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

11. Modern Irrigation: Techniques like drip irrigation and sprinklers reduce water wastage by delivering water directly to the plant's root zone, minimizing evaporation and surface runoff. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

12. Road Transport in Northern Plains: Road transport is well developed in the northern plains primarily because the terrain is flat, allowing for easy construction and maintenance of road networks. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

13. Rail vs. Air Transport: Transport by railways is often more beneficial than airways in India because railways can transport heavy and bulky goods over long distances at a relatively lower cost. Railways also have a much wider reach across the country compared to the limited connectivity of airways. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

14. Painting of Trees: Painting the base of trees in industrial areas is made mandatory because the presence of trees helps absorb carbon dioxide and regulates air quality. It also helps in filtering pollutants and reducing dust pollution caused by industrial activity. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

15. Waste Segregation: Waste must be segregated at source before disposal to facilitate efficient recycling and reuse of materials. Segregation prevents the mixing of hazardous waste with general waste, reducing environmental damage and making disposal methods safer. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

16. Spoilage of Landscape: Spoilage of landscape is harmful because it can destroy the natural aesthetic and ecological balance of an area. This can negatively impact tourism, destroy habitats, and contribute to pollution when unsightly dumps or mining remnants are left exposed. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

17. Handloom Industry Role: The handloom industry is important as it provides significant employment, especially in rural areas, playing a role in the Indian economy. It also preserves traditional skills and crafts, contributing to India's cultural heritage. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

Section B: Definitions, Classification, and Factual Recall

18. Soil Erosion & Techniques: Soil erosion is the wearing away of the top layer of soil. Modern techniques for erosion control include terracing on hillsides and contour ploughing. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

19. Monsoon Roles: The summer monsoon is critical for supplying water for agriculture and for hydro power generation in India. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

20. Waste Accumulation and Air Quality: The accumulation of waste impacts air quality by releasing toxic gases and foul odour into the atmosphere, which can spread diseases. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

21. Biosafety: Biosafety is defined as the procedures used for the safe handling and disposal of biological or hazardous materials. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

22. Biomagnification: Biomagnification is the process where the concentration of a substance increases in organisms at successively higher levels in a food chain. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

23. Biomagnification Example: An example of a substance undergoing Biomagnification is Mercury or DDT (if present in industrial or agricultural runoff). — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

24. Heavy vs. Light Industry: Heavy Industry deals with large, bulky raw materials and products (e.g., Iron and Steel Industry). Light Industry deals with lightweight raw materials and products (e.g., Electronic Industry). — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

25. Plantation Crop Challenges:

  • Economic Challenge: Market volatility and fluctuating export prices.
  • Environmental Challenge: Requires extensive use of monoculture, which can lead to soil depletion and pest proliferation.
— Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

26. Steps to Boost Agriculture: Steps taken by the government include providing better irrigation facilities and offering credit/loan facilities to farmers. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

27. Nuclear Mineral: Uranium (or Thorium) is crucial for generating nuclear power. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

28. Coal as Strategic Resource: Coal is considered a strategic resource because it is the most important source of energy in India. It is widely available and is used as a fuel in thermal power plants. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

29. Coal Mining Impact: A major ecological impact of coal mining in Central India is the destruction of forest cover and resulting spoilage of the landscape. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

30. Bauxite Uses: The metal extracted from bauxite (Aluminium) is used in making aircraft parts and electrical wires/cables. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

31. Tropical Rain Forest Characteristics: These forests have dense growth and tall trees that form a continuous canopy. They typically shed their leaves at different times, remaining evergreen. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

32. Saline Coastal Vegetation: Mangrove forests are the type of vegetation found in saline coastal areas of Gujarat. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

33. Mangroves as Natural Barriers: Mangrove forests act as natural barriers against disasters like cyclones because their dense root systems help stabilize the coastline and reduce the intensity of storm surges. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

34. Forests as Climate Stabilizers:

  • Forests prevent surface runoff, allowing water to percolate and recharge groundwater supplies (soil conservation).
  • They reduce high surface temperatures through evapotranspiration, moderating the local climate.
— Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

35. Tube Well Conditions: Geographical conditions include a high water table and soft alluvial soil that is easy to bore. Economic conditions include the availability of electricity and low pumping costs. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

36. Canals vs. Wells: Perennial canals are highly reliable as they draw water directly from permanent rivers, ensuring supply even in dry seasons. Surface wells are less reliable as they depend heavily on the local water table, which can drop during droughts, making canals more sustainable in the long term. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

37. Canal Irrigation States: Punjab and Uttar Pradesh are two states where canal irrigation is vital. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

38. Biogas and Rural Energy: Biogas contributes to rural energy security by providing a clean source of fuel for cooking and lighting, reducing reliance on firewood and preventing deforestation. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

39. Tidal vs. Wind Energy: Tidal energy is highly reliable and predictable because the tides follow fixed astronomical cycles. Wind energy is less reliable as it is intermittent and depends on highly variable wind speeds. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

40. Golden Quadrilateral Objectives: The project aims to connect major metropolitan cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata). It also aims to reduce travel time and transport costs between these hubs. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

41. National Waterways-1 Terminals: The terminal cities are Haldia and Allahabad. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

42. Inland Waterways Advantages: They are economical for transporting heavy and bulky goods. They are also more environmentally friendly compared to road transport. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish

44. Effects of Acid Rain: Acid rain harms vegetation and forests. It accelerates the corrosion of metal structures and damages monuments and buildings. It also pollutes aquatic ecosystems, harming fish and other life. — Compiled by Sriyans and Devashish
