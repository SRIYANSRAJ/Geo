Section A: Analytical and Geographical Reasoning

1. Coastal Regions Cooler: Coastal regions experience relatively cooler days due to the moderating effect of the sea. Water heats up and cools down slower than land, influencing temperature extremes.

2. Aravalli Failure: The Aravalli Hills fail to intercept monsoon winds effectively because they run parallel to the direction of the southwest monsoon winds, allowing the moisture-laden air to pass by without shedding much rain.

3. Khadar vs. Bangar:

  • Khadar is the new, younger alluvial soil, generally found in the flood plains, which is highly fertile and suitable for intensive agriculture.
  • Bangar is the older alluvial soil, found away from the flood plains, which is less fertile, often containing calcareous nodules, influencing crop selection towards less water-intensive varieties.

4. Black Soil & Cotton: Black soil is rich in minerals and has a high clay content, which gives it an excellent capacity for moisture retention. Cotton requires high temperatures, light rainfall (or irrigation), and a large number of frost-free days for proper growth.

5. Chennai and Varanasi Temp Range: Chennai, being coastal, experiences a lower annual temperature range due to a maritime climate. Varanasi, being inland, experiences a higher annual temperature range due to a continental climate.

6. Tank Irrigation Reasons: Tank irrigation is most prevalent in the peninsular region because the topography consists of hard, impermeable rock, which is ideal for collecting and storing water. Additionally, the seasonal nature of rainfall (monsoon dependence) necessitates the collection of water during wet months.

7. Chota Nagpur Plateau Hub: The Chota Nagpur Plateau region has become a hub of the steel industry due to its proximity to vital raw materials like iron ore and coal.

8. Silk Industry in Karnataka: The silk industry in Karnataka is well developed partly because the region provides favourable climatic conditions for rearing silkworms. Furthermore, the industry benefits from the traditional skills and labour availability in the region.

9. Limitations of Transplantation:

  • Transplantation is extremely labour-intensive, requiring a high density of workers, which can be costly and challenging in areas facing rural labour shortages.
  • The method is time-consuming and often limits the rapid adoption of modern technology necessary for large-scale efficient farming.

10. Hindrances to Mechanization: Rural poverty means farmers often lack the capital needed to invest in expensive machinery and modern equipment. Land fragmentation (small, scattered landholdings) makes the use of large agricultural machinery economically unviable and physically difficult.

11. Modern Irrigation: Techniques like drip irrigation and sprinklers reduce water wastage by delivering water directly to the plant's root zone, minimizing evaporation and surface runoff.

12. Road Transport in Northern Plains: Road transport is well developed in the northern plains primarily because the terrain is flat, allowing for easy construction and maintenance of road networks.

13. Rail vs. Air Transport: Transport by railways is often more beneficial than airways in India because railways can transport heavy and bulky goods over long distances at a relatively lower cost. Railways also have a much wider reach across the country compared to the limited connectivity of airways.

14. Painting of Trees: Painting the base of trees in industrial areas is made mandatory because the presence of trees helps absorb carbon dioxide and regulates air quality. It also helps in filtering pollutants and reducing dust pollution caused by industrial activity.

15. Waste Segregation: Waste must be segregated at source before disposal to facilitate efficient recycling and reuse of materials. Segregation prevents the mixing of hazardous waste with general waste, reducing environmental damage and making disposal methods safer.

16. Spoilage of Landscape: Spoilage of landscape is harmful because it can destroy the natural aesthetic and ecological balance of an area. This can negatively impact tourism, destroy habitats, and contribute to pollution when unsightly dumps or mining remnants are left exposed.

17. Handloom Industry Role: The handloom industry is important as it provides significant employment, especially in rural areas, playing a role in the Indian economy. It also preserves traditional skills and crafts, contributing to India's cultural heritage.

Section B: Definitions, Classification, and Factual Recall

18. Soil Erosion & Techniques: Soil erosion is the wearing away of the top layer of soil. Modern techniques for erosion control include terracing on hillsides and contour ploughing.

19. Monsoon Roles: The summer monsoon is critical for supplying water for agriculture and for hydro power generation in India.

20. Waste Accumulation and Air Quality: The accumulation of waste impacts air quality by releasing toxic gases and foul odour into the atmosphere, which can spread diseases.

21. Biosafety: Biosafety is defined as the procedures used for the safe handling and disposal of biological or hazardous materials.

22. Biomagnification: Biomagnification is the process where the concentration of a substance increases in organisms at successively higher levels in a food chain.

23. Biomagnification Example: An example of a substance undergoing Biomagnification is Mercury or DDT (if present in industrial or agricultural runoff).

24. Heavy vs. Light Industry: Heavy Industry deals with large, bulky raw materials and products (e.g., Iron and Steel Industry). Light Industry deals with lightweight raw materials and products (e.g., Electronic Industry).

25. Plantation Crop Challenges:

  • Economic Challenge: Market volatility and fluctuating export prices.
  • Environmental Challenge: Requires extensive use of monoculture, which can lead to soil depletion and pest proliferation.

26. Steps to Boost Agriculture: Steps taken by the government include providing better irrigation facilities and offering credit/loan facilities to farmers.

27. Nuclear Mineral: Uranium (or Thorium) is crucial for generating nuclear power.

28. Coal as Strategic Resource: Coal is considered a strategic resource because it is the most important source of energy in India. It is widely available and is used as a fuel in thermal power plants.

29. Coal Mining Impact: A major ecological impact of coal mining in Central India is the destruction of forest cover and resulting spoilage of the landscape.

30. Bauxite Uses: The metal extracted from bauxite (Aluminium) is used in making aircraft parts and electrical wires/cables.

31. Tropical Rain Forest Characteristics: These forests have dense growth and tall trees that form a continuous canopy. They typically shed their leaves at different times, remaining evergreen.

32. Saline Coastal Vegetation: Mangrove forests are the type of vegetation found in saline coastal areas of Gujarat.

33. Mangroves as Natural Barriers: Mangrove forests act as natural barriers against disasters like cyclones because their dense root systems help stabilize the coastline and reduce the intensity of storm surges.

34. Forests as Climate Stabilizers:

  • Forests prevent surface runoff, allowing water to percolate and recharge groundwater supplies (soil conservation).
  • They reduce high surface temperatures through evapotranspiration, moderating the local climate.

35. Tube Well Conditions: Geographical conditions include a high water table and soft alluvial soil that is easy to bore. Economic conditions include the availability of electricity and low pumping costs.

36. Canals vs. Wells: Perennial canals are highly reliable as they draw water directly from permanent rivers, ensuring supply even in dry seasons. Surface wells are less reliable as they depend heavily on the local water table, which can drop during droughts, making canals more sustainable in the long term.

37. Canal Irrigation States: Punjab and Uttar Pradesh are two states where canal irrigation is vital.

38. Biogas and Rural Energy: Biogas contributes to rural energy security by providing a clean source of fuel for cooking and lighting, reducing reliance on firewood and preventing deforestation.

39. Tidal vs. Wind Energy: Tidal energy is highly reliable and predictable because the tides follow fixed astronomical cycles. Wind energy is less reliable as it is intermittent and depends on highly variable wind speeds.

40. Golden Quadrilateral Objectives: The project aims to connect major metropolitan cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata). It also aims to reduce travel time and transport costs between these hubs.

41. National Waterways-1 Terminals: The terminal cities are Haldia and Allahabad.

42. Inland Waterways Advantages: They are economical for transporting heavy and bulky goods. They are also more environmentally friendly compared to road transport.

43. River Systems for Waterways: The Ganga River system (for NW-1) and the Brahmaputra River system (for NW-2) are utilized for inland waterways.

44. Effects of Acid Rain: Acid rain harms vegetation and forests. It accelerates the corrosion of metal structures and damages monuments and buildings. It also pollutes aquatic ecosystems, harming fish and other life.

Section C: Integrated Industry and Agricultural Specifics

45. Alluvial Soil Characteristics: Alluvial soil is rich in humus and highly fertile. It is well-drained and ideal for a variety of crops.

46. Soil in Western Ghats: Laterite soil is predominantly found in the Western Ghats. Its limitation is that it possesses poor fertility, making it less suitable for basic food crops, though it supports tea and coffee.

47. Black Soil vs. Laterite Soil Comparison:

  • Similarities: Both are rich in iron and aluminium compounds. Both are used for growing commercial crops (Black for cotton, Laterite for tea/coffee).
  • Difference: Black soil is rich in minerals and highly fertile, while Laterite soil has poor fertility.

48. Manganese Importance and Challenges: Manganese is critical for the steel industry as it is required in the manufacturing process. A challenge is maintaining self-sufficiency, ensuring adequate reserves and production to meet the demands of the critical steel sector.

49. Bhilai Steel Plant Location: It is located in the state of Chhattisgarh.

50. Bhilai Collaborator: Soviet Russia collaborated with India to build the Bhilai Steel Plant.

51. Bhilai Manganese Supply: It gets its supply of Manganese from the state of Madhya Pradesh.

52. Bhilai Rail Route: It is located on the Mumbai-Kolkata rail route.

53. Bhilai Power Supply: It gets its main power supply from the Korba Thermal Power Station.

54. Bhilai Product: The Bhilai Steel Plant manufactures rail materials (rail tracks).

55. Dual Rainfall Region: A region receiving rainfall from both the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches is typically the northeastern part of the Deccan Plateau or the central Gangetic plains.

56. Northeast Rainfall: The Bay of Bengal branch of the monsoon is responsible for rainfall in northeastern India.

57. Tamil Nadu Rainfall: The Tamil Nadu coast receives most of its rainfall during the retreating monsoon (or northeast monsoon). It lies in the rain shadow area of the Western Ghats during the southwest monsoon.

58. Crop Seasons: (a) Wheat: Rabi, (b) Rice: Kharif, (c) Watermelon: Zaid.

59. Waterlogging Resistant Tree: A tree species resistant to waterlogging, used in boat-making, is Sundri (or Mangrove species).

60. Exported Tropical Hardwood: A tropical hardwood used in making expensive furniture and commonly exported is Teak.

61. High Altitude Hardwood: A hardwood tree found at higher altitudes, valued for construction, is Deodar.

62. Winter Rainfall Region: Tamil Nadu (Coromandel) receives winter rainfall.
